What this is

Every pipe here is a real model of a stopped flue pipe: a tube closed at the foot and open at the top. A closed tube can only hold odd quarter-waves, so

pitch = speed of sound ÷ (4 × length)

…and that one line is responsible for everything you are about to hear.

Things to try

  • Drag up from the block to cut a pipe. Longer means lower, and twice as long is an octave lower: length is in the denominator.
  • Touch a pipe to blow it, and hold to keep it sounding.
  • Drag a pipe sideways to change its bore. A wider mouth makes the pipe acoustically longer, so a fat pipe sits flat of its measured length.
  • Drag across a pipe's mouth — the brass lip. Low gives a flutey tone, high a stringy one: where the windhole sits decides which partials get excited.
  • Flick a pipe up hard to overblow it. It jumps a twelfth (3×), not an octave — the signature of a pipe closed at the foot, which has no even partials. The wave drawn inside gains a node when it happens.
  • Turn tune-lock off, cut two pipes a few cents apart, and blow both: you hear the beat, which is exactly the difference between them.
  • Warm the air with the slider. Sound travels faster in warm air, so the whole rack goes sharp together.
